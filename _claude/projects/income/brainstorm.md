## All Ideas — Sorted by Time to First Money

### ⚡ Immediate (days)

#### Sell things you own
- Declutter and sell on Blocket.se or Facebook Marketplace
- Electronics, clothes, games, anything collecting dust
- Zero investment, fast cash, one-time
- Notes: Good first move to generate seed capital for other ideas

#### Bottle returns (Pant)
- Sweden's deposit system: 1 SEK (small) to 2 SEK (large) per bottle/can
- Collect from home, ask family/neighbors, check recycling areas
- Tiny amounts per session but zero effort and completely free money
- Notes: Don't build a strategy around this — but don't ignore it either

#### Odd jobs (local)
- Snow shoveling (very relevant in Värmland winters), lawn mowing, moving help, cleaning
- Direct offer to neighbors, Facebook local groups, Grannhjälpen, Nextdoor
- Fastest possible local money — no platform needed
- Ceiling: limited by local demand and season

### 🟢 Fast (1–4 weeks)

#### Part-time job
- **Most reliable and fastest path to consistent income for your situation**
- Sweden has strong worker protections — even part-time hours add up significantly
- Hourly rate for 16-17: ~80-100 SEK/hr, 18+: ~120-140 SEK/hr (varies)
- 5 hrs/week = ~600 SEK/month minimum. 10 hrs/week = ~1200 SEK/month
- Hagfors options: ICA, Willys, Systembolaget, restaurants, gas stations
- Notes: This is the unsexy answer that actually works. Fastest to consistent positive cash flow.

#### Reselling (flip items)
- Buy underpriced items, sell for profit
- Sources: Blocket.se, Facebook Marketplace, thrift stores (Myrorna, Röda Korset), garage sales
- Sell on: Tradera.com (Swedish eBay), Blocket, Vinted (clothes), Sellpy
- Best niches: electronics, vintage clothes, sports equipment, LEGO, branded items
- Capital needed: $20–100 to start. Scales with experience.
- Notes: Requires a good eye and research. Vinted for clothes is especially low friction.

#### Sell skills locally
- Flyers or Facebook posts: computer help, tech support for older residents
- Hagfors has a significant older population — real demand for basic tech help
- No platform needed. Direct cash.

### 🟡 Medium (1–3 months to first meaningful money)

#### Freelance Web Development
- Build sites, apps, or tools for small businesses or remote clients
- Platforms: Upwork, Fiverr, PeoplePerHour, Frilans.nu (SE)
- Portfolio: Mise + Empty PWA + VALE are real, polished demos
- Realistic first income: 2–6 weeks after first proposal accepted
- Notes: Requires good proposal writing and patience for first reviews

#### Print-on-Demand
- Design graphics → sell on products (shirts, mugs, posters) — no inventory, no upfront cost
- Platforms: Redbubble, Merch by Amazon (invite-based), Printify + Etsy
- Zero capital required. Passive once listings are live.
- Volume game — needs many listings and some luck with algorithm
- Niches that work: humor, gaming, local/regional pride, niche hobbies
- Notes: AI tools (Midjourney, etc.) have lowered the design barrier significantly

#### Digital Products (templates, files, tools)
- Notion templates, Figma templates, Excel/Google Sheets tools, desktop wallpapers, icons
- Sell on: Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Creative Market, Etsy
- Make once, sell forever
- Your React/Vite/Supabase starter templates fit here perfectly
- Notes: Niche matters more than quality. Generic templates don't sell.

#### Affiliate Marketing
- Promote products/services online, earn commission on sales
- Works best with an audience (blog, social media, YouTube, Reddit)
- Programs: Amazon Associates, Awin (SE-friendly), Impact, PartnerStack
- Niches: tech, software tools, web hosting, gaming
- Realistic without audience: very slow. With audience: passive at scale.
- Notes: Not a standalone strategy without traffic. Good complement to content.

#### Content Creation (TikTok / Instagram)
- Short-form video has the fastest growth curve right now
- Monetization: brand deals (first, before ads), affiliate links, products
- Brand deals are realistic at 5K–10K followers in the right niche
- Tech, programming, AI, day-in-the-life — all viable angles
- Notes: Takes 3–6 months to see traction. High variance. Fun if you enjoy it.

### 🟠 Slow (3–12 months to meaningful income)

#### YouTube — Traditional
- Build a channel around a real interest or skill (dev, AI, gaming, tech reviews, whatever)
- Ad revenue (YPP) needs 1000 subs + 4000 watch hours. Realistically 6–18 months.
- Income before YPP: sponsorships and affiliate (possible at smaller scale in right niche)
- Notes: Needs genuine interest in the topic. Burn rate is very real.

#### YouTube Automation / Faceless Channel
- Outsource or AI-generate scripts, voiceover, and editing
- Niches: top 10 lists, finance, history, true crime, motivational
- Capital needed: $100–500/month to run properly (outsourced content costs)
- Competition is fierce but it still works in underserved niches
- Notes: Hard to start with $400. Easier once you have a cash flow from something else.

#### Dropshipping
- List products in an online store; supplier ships directly to buyer — you keep margin
- Platforms: Shopify + AliExpress/Zendrop, or TikTok Shop
- Realistic margins: 20–40% after platform fees and ads
- **The reality:** very competitive. Most beginners lose money on ads before finding a winner.
- Works best: niche products with emotional appeal, tested with small ad budget
- Capital needed: $200+ to test properly. Not recommended as a first step with $400 savings.
- Notes: Works for people who treat it seriously and test systematically. Not passive.

#### Micro-SaaS
- Small focused web tool solving a specific problem, monetised by subscription
- Stack fit: high — React + Vite + Supabase is exactly right
- Realistic timeline to $1 MRR: 2–6 months (build + find customers)
- Notes: Distribution is the hard part. The tool is the easy part.

#### AI Niche / Consulting
- Local AI setup, Ollama, LLM tooling — growing demand, few people who can help
- Format: freelance consulting, async help via Discord/Reddit, written guides
- VALE is direct proof of capability
- Notes: Niche but legitimate. Better as a complement than standalone.

#### Open Source + Sponsorship
- Publish something genuinely useful; monetise via GitHub Sponsors, Polar.sh, ko-fi
- Long game. Builds audience and credibility.
- Notes: Not fast. Worth doing anyway if something is worth sharing.

### 🔴 Very slow / Long-term bets

#### Stock / Index Fund Investing
- Invest savings in low-cost index funds (Avanza, Nordnet in Sweden)
- With $400: realistic long-term, not income now
- Expected return: 7–10% annually — on $400 that's $28–40/year
- Notes: Do this anyway as a habit. Not an income strategy at current savings level.

#### Courses / Teaching
- Create and sell structured learning — video courses, written guides
- Platforms: Gumroad, Teachable, Skillshare, Udemy
- Requires established credibility or audience first
